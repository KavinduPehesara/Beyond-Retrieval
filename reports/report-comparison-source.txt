Executive Summary 
The number of research papers is increasing at a rate that results in two burdens: first, to 
choose papers that satisfy specific criteria; and, second, to find what research has yet to be 
performed. The first has 20 years of automation research and the second has virtually none. 
The first seems to be addressed by LLMs, as several studies report 95–100% screening 
recall. The results are debated: Khraisha et al. (2024) reported a sensitivity of 0.42 for balanced 
data, and Hida et al. (2026) observed accuracy differences as large as 47 percentage points 
among the models. Three gaps follow. Capability has been absent from becoming open and 
evaluated systems, with only four of the twenty-one surveyed tools in open source, and these are 
not extensively used yet (Bolaños et al., 2024). Cost and run-to-run variation is uncharacterised. 
No one knows what is not in a literature. 
The idea for this project is an open, self-running dashboard that the researcher can check 
output of it instead of taking for granted. All decisions are verbatim quotes of the source, and all 
decisions are automatically checked; repeated runs are tested for consistency; and all decisions 
are made by a human. The system also identifies potential gaps by grouping together the 
restrictions that authors mention in their papers. 
Evaluation is based on a six-review subset of SYNERGY with inclusion rates ranging 
from 0.8% to 21.9%, compared to four baselines. It is a reproducible contribution, and a first 
treatment of gap discovery as a measurable task.

1. Introduction 
There is a great burden of published research, and it grows faster than any individual can 
keep up with. The first is screening which involves making decisions about which papers in a 
large candidate set fulfil the inclusion criteria. The second, less popular, is to detect what has not 
yet been explored – a process that requires careful reading to notice a lack. Misidentified gaps 
result in wasted effort and to proposals that are not novelty-proofed, and screening takes months 
of reviewer time that would be better used in synthesis. 
The first burden is 20 years of automated research, whereas the second burden has just 
about zero. This project adapts to that imbalance by developing and testing a system that can 
screen and simultaneously uncover the gaps, based on verifiable evidence for each decision; to 
make auditable review practice available to individual researchers; and to view gap discovery as 
a measurable engineering problem.

2. Research Method — Systematic Literature Review 
This evidence base is created using Kitchenham and Charters (2007) as the basis and 
following the systematic reviews of literature.The evidence base is created using the reference 
standard for evidence-based software engineering, Kitchenham and Charters (2007), and 
following systematic review of literature. It is selected for two reasons: One is that this project 
argues against the existence of research gaps, requiring that the search be documented and 
repeatable; and two, it is a contested field, meaning that unsystematic reading would mean 
selecting the evidence that is relevant to the claim. 
2.1 Search Strategy and Study Selection 
The sources searched for complementary coverage are the system's own data source 
OpenAlex, as well as Scopus and Web of Science for curated indexing, IEEE Xplore for 
software engineering venues, and the ACM Digital Library for software engineering venues; 
arXiv for preprints. Selection is a 2-stage process, from the title and abstract up to Section 2.4 
and from full text of survivors to snowballing, and number of records reported in a PRISMA 
flow diagram. 
2.2 Synthesis of Literature 
Studies included are thematically synthesized, not statistically, due to the diversity of 
study designs and the variety of study data sets and outcome measures. The studies are grouped 
by four themes: screening automation; language model performance; retrieval and 
representation; and identification of explicit limitations. The case for the claim is contrasted with 
the evaluation design that underpins the claim, and with the impact reported — getting at the

3. Analysis 
There are two levels of analysis: Thematic coding of literature is carried out based on the 
four themes mentioned above, the characteristics of the studies include the study claim, 
evaluation design, the data used, the balance of the class and the effects that can be obtained. 
Class balance is deliberately recorded: Khraisha et al. (2024) demonstrated that accuracy scores 
are prone to reporting inaccuracies when the included-to-excluded ratio is skewed; and Kusa et 
al. (2023) demonstrated that the default efficiency metric is not comparable because of the same 
reason. 
An evaluation harness is created as part of the system to analyse experimental data. Due 
to the significant imbalance in the datasets, performance is reported as Recall at fixed thresholds 
and the True Negative rate at 95% Recall, with the latter being the same measure shown in the 
Work Saved over Sampling graph in Kusa et al. (2023) which was found to be comparable 
across reviews. Inter run agreement (IRA) is based on Gwet's AC2 which is more robust against 
imbalance than Cohen's kappa. 
Interpretation is supported by a prompt sensitivity analysis, a manual error analysis of 
fifty misclassified records and an ablation study. All analysis was performed by using committed 
configuration files and each figure can be reproduced from the configuration files.

4. Gap Identification 
Review of twenty sources identified three gaps. 
Gap 1: Demonstrated capability has not become open, evaluated systems. 
Bolaños et al. (2024) surveyed twenty-one review tools against thirty-four features and 
found only four open-licensed and none supporting citation snowballing. Assessment, they 
conclude, is obstructed by "the absence of standard evaluation frameworks and established 
benchmarks," compounded because commercial providers withhold evaluation data. A 
researcher cannot determine the recall of the tool they rely on. 
Gap 2: Cost and reproducibility are uncharacterised. 
Hida et al. (2026) argue model selection should rest on operational criteria — cost, 
transparency, reproducibility — rather than aggregate performance, then state no cost–benefit 
analysis was performed in their own study. They also show decisions vary between identical runs 
at temperature zero, with agreement between 0.55 and 1.00. 
Gap 3: Identification of absent research is unaddressed. 
None of the thirty-four features assessed by Bolaños et al. (2024) concerns gap 
identification or future-work extraction. Hu and Wan (2015) and Zhang et al. (2023) show 
authors’ statements about unresolved problems can be extracted and classified, Zhang et al. 
reaching macro F1 of 90.73%, but this has never been connected to review tooling. Their 
weakest category is problem, at F1 43.64% — the class corresponding to an unresolved gap.

5. Problem Statement 
The three gaps come together on one problem. The candidate sets are very large, and the 
researchers have to sift through them manually in order to find the desired research.There is no 
instrument that shows what a literature does not contain and very large candidate sets, which 
have to be screened by the researchers manually. There are 169,288 papers in a public dataset 
constructed from 26 real systematic reviews, and only 1.67% were kept in the final set, about 
fifty-nine papers that were irrelevant. To identify something that is not studied is even more 
difficult and more expensive, as it takes months to get it wrong on a question that has already 
been answered. 
While it seems like language models solve the first half, the results are contained within 
published research, not something that a researcher can use and check. Tools such as Elicit and 
Consensus are closed, so their accuracy is unknown. All are designed to discover papers which 
are present; none indicate what is missing. 
The problem is that screening decisions based on a language model can be neither 
checked by the researcher relying on the model, nor can a literature be transformed into a 
statement of what has not been studied. One research question below is addressed per each half.

8. Methodology 
8.1 Research design 
The project is undertaken using the Design Science Research, as the contribution is an 
artefact, not a theory that is tested through observation, but against a set of criteria. The five 
activities outlined below correspond directly to the project: problem identification (Section 2), 
objectives (Section 7), design and development, demonstration on a benchmark corpus, and 
evaluation (Section 10). It is better than an experimental design because artefact and evaluation 
are interwoven and better than action research because there is no organisational intervention. 
Progress is made over a week at a time, with weeks 1-6 finished. There are two 
intentional sequencing decisions. In week 7 a thin end-to-end implementation is constructed, 
without any optimisation done so far, and in week 8 an evaluation harness is added that yields a 
reportable number, hence any optimisation in later weeks will yield a reportable number. Gap 
discovery is a decision point not a deadline: If it does not satisfy the criteria in Section 10, the 
remainder of the effort goes into prior-art retrieval which also addresses RQ2.

8.2 Data collection and evaluation corpus 
Bibliographic data is provided by the OpenAlex API, an open API that provides abstract, 
citations, institutional metadata. The label sets are provided by the SYNERGY project, which 
was published by the ASReview project: 206,928 records and 26 labelled reviews. 
The full benchmark requires approximately twenty-five US dollars to screen and the 
design requires about twenty passes. This is beyond budget and would require giving up the 
repeated-run measurement RQ1 relies on. Evaluation thus takes the six-reviewed set in Table 6, 
for which the inclusion rates vary between 0.8% and 21.9% (Khraisha et al. 2024 demonstrated 
that accuracy rises with prevalence rates that span this range, but a larger corpus would not have 
done so quite as much).

8.4 Ethical and cultural considerations 
Designing four considerations. Provenance: Since citations made can be verified as 
legitimate, one study determined that 19.9% of generated citations were actually fabricated, and 
every claim has a verbatim span that can be matched with the source. Indexing bias: OpenAlex 
covers the geography of the research landscape but is not evenly distributed across languages and 
regions. Recommendations (automated bias): All decisions are recommendations with evidence 
(that is visible) and can be turned off. Responsible use: Data is used from public data sources 
according to their terms, and ethical approval is secured before contacting the participants.

9. Proposed Solution 
The solution is a locally runnable dashboard, a layered modular monolith, that takes a 
research question and inclusion criteria and returns a screened, evidenced and mapped body of 
literature with candidate research gaps. 
9.1 Services 
There are 6 services in the background that are documented via an API (Figure 1). 
Normalises OpenAlex records and ingests them. Document embeddings and a lexical index are 
computed by indexing. Retrieval is the combination and reordering of rankings and reordering 
using a cross-encoder. Screening uses a language model to apply the inclusion criteria and 
returns a decision, the confidence score and the supporting sentence, while also caching all 
responses and tracking the cost of each of these responses. Gap analysis generates statements of 
what is missing or what needs to be done, categorizes them based on the categories listed by 
Zhang et al. (2023), and visualizes a coverage matrix where the sparse cells represent possible 
gaps.

9.4 Design decisions and scope 
Only abstracts are used for screening, and removing the abstracts would cost 5.55 
percentage points of accuracy, while removing the titles and keywords would have no effect. 
Future-work statements are found in conclusions, hence the asymmetric depth: abstracts in the 
full corpus, open access full text only in the subset. The evaluation harness uses services directly, 
and does not go through the interface at all, thus each result is repeatable from a configuration 
file. 
The principle of scope is that a smaller system that is well evaluated is better than a larger 
system that is not. So core scope is objectives 1-10, no model training or deployment 
infrastructure. Graph views and non-English screening are stretch and droppable without causing 
any loss of results.
